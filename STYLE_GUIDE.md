# Editorial Style Guide

**The Father's House, All Nations Church — Port Moresby, Papua New Guinea**

This guide governs all written copy on this website. Its purpose is a single,
consistent voice: **governmental, professional, and authoritative** in register,
and **exact** in the way it handles Scripture — quoting the text the way counsel
stands on a statute.

Editors: when copy and this guide disagree, the guide prevails. When a fact and
this guide disagree, the fact prevails — never bend a name, date, or Scripture
reference to fit the style.

---

## 1. Voice in one sentence

We write as a standing house issuing its record: measured, declarative, and
unhurried, resting its claims on the authority of Scripture rather than on
volume or excitement.

---

## 2. The three registers, held together

| Register | What it means here | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Governmental** | The house speaks as an institution with a mandate, not a personality with a mood. Prefer the settled statement of what *is* over the appeal to feeling. | Could this sentence sit in a charter or a proclamation without embarrassment? |
| **Professional** | Clean, precise, and disciplined. No slang, no filler, no exclamation as a substitute for weight. | Remove every word and see if the sentence still stands. If it does, the word was filler. |
| **Authoritative** | Statements are made, not hedged. We do not "think," "hope," or "feel"; we hold, affirm, declare, and stand. | Does the sentence assert, or does it apologise for asserting? |

These three are one voice, not three. Authority without professionalism is
noise; the professional tone without the governmental frame reads like a
brochure. Keep all three in every paragraph.

---

## 3. How we cite Scripture — the standard of counsel

Scripture is the warrant on which this house acts. Cite it the way a lawyer
cites the law it is standing on: **name the text, quote it exactly, mark the
translation, and let it carry the weight.** The verse is the authority; the
prose around it only points to it.

### Citation form

> **"…then will I hear from heaven … and will heal their land."**
> — 2 Chronicles 7:14 (KJV)

Rules:

1. **Reference is mandatory and precise.** Always Book, chapter, verse:
   *Mark 11:17*, not "Mark" or "the Gospels." A quotation without its reference
   is an assertion without its authority — never publish one.
2. **Name the translation** in parentheses after the reference — **(KJV)**,
   **(NIV)**, **(ESV)** — on first and prominent uses. This is the equivalent of
   citing the edition of the statute. Do not silently mix translations inside a
   single quoted sentence.
3. **Quote exactly.** Reproduce the wording of the named translation verbatim,
   including its punctuation. Use an ellipsis (…) to mark any omission honestly;
   never compress or paraphrase inside quotation marks.
4. **Distinguish quotation from paraphrase.** If the wording is ours, drop the
   quotation marks and do not attach a bare reference as though it were a quote.
   A loose restatement may be *grounded in* a passage — write "on the pattern of
   Matthew 22:37" — but it is not *quoting* it.
5. **Let the verse stand; do not gloss it.** Introduce with a verb of authority
   — *Scripture declares… It is written… The record states…* — then quote, then
   stop. Resist explaining the verse in the same breath; the citation does the
   work.
6. **Placement.** Set a governing verse apart as a block citation (as above)
   rather than folding it mid-sentence, so it reads as the authority it is.

### Verbs of authority (introducing Scripture)

Prefer: *Scripture declares · It is written · The Word affirms · The record
states · As it stands written · On the authority of.*

Avoid: *the Bible kind of says · this verse talks about · a nice reminder
that.*

## 4. Tone: what to prefer, what to retire

**Prefer**

- Declarative sentences that state the house's position: *"This house holds
  that prayer is its first work."*
- Institutional subjects: *This house · The Father's House, All Nations Church ·
  This congregation · The leadership of this house.*
- Verbs of standing and commitment: *holds, affirms, stands, commits, contends,
  convenes, declares, upholds.*
- Full, correct proper nouns and titles on first mention (see §6).

**Retire**

- Exclamation marks as emphasis. Weight comes from the claim, not the
  punctuation. Reserve, at most, one signature exception (see §5).
- ALL-CAPS shouting for emphasis. Capitalise names and titles, not moods.
- Hype and filler: *amazing, super, incredible, totally, just, honestly,
  literally.*
- Second-person hard sell: rephrase "You need to be there!" as an invitation or
  a stated fact — *"The house convenes for prayer each Friday evening."*
- Rhetorical questions used as filler. A genuine, answerable question is fine
  (*"How may this house pray with you?"*).

---

## 5. The signature line

The mandate line — *Executing the Mandate. Advancing the Kingdom.* — is retained
as the house's standing motto. In this register it is written as a measured
decree, not a shout:

> **Executing the Mandate. Advancing the Kingdom. It is now.**

- Set it as three short declarative clauses, sentence case, closed with a full
  stop.
- Do **not** render it in all-caps or with an exclamation mark in body copy.
- Use it deliberately — as a closing seal on a section — not repeatedly within
  one passage.

---

## 6. Names, titles, and the record of fact

Accuracy of the institutional record is part of the authority. Reproduce these
exactly; never abbreviate or approximate them in published copy.

- **The Father's House, All Nations Church** — full name on first use in a
  section. "This house" or "TFH ANC" is acceptable thereafter.
- **The Father's House Inc.** — IPA-registered entity, registration **5-109620**,
  registered **31 March 2021**. Cite the registration precisely when the corporate
  identity is stated.
- **Pastor Ben Minok** — Founder and Senior Elder of The Father's House Inc.;
  Senior Pastor of All Nations Church, Port Moresby. Titles precede the name on
  first mention.
- **Dr Jonathan David** — Presiding Apostle of the International Strategic
  Alliance of Apostolic Churches (**ISAAC**). Spell the network out on first use,
  then "the ISAAC Network."
- **Dates** — day month year, in full: *9 April 2021*, *20 April 2021*. Do not
  use numeric-only dates.
- **Places** — *Taurama Aquatic Centre Lounge, Port Moresby, Papua New Guinea.*
  Spell "Papua New Guinea" in full at least once per page before using "PNG."

## 7. Mechanics

- **Spelling:** Commonwealth / British English (*honour, programme, centre,
  recognise, baptised*), consistent with Papua New Guinea usage.
- **Times:** 12-hour clock with uppercase meridiem — *7:00 PM*, *9:00 AM –
  1:30 PM*. Use an en dash for ranges.
- **Capitalisation of the sacred:** capitalise *God, the Lord, Scripture, the
  Word, the Gospel, the Kingdom, the Name,* and pronouns for God (*He, His, Him*).
- **Numbers:** spell out one to nine in prose; use figures for 10 and above, and
  always for dates, times, ages, and registration numbers.
- **Emphasis:** use bold sparingly for the single load-bearing phrase in a
  passage. Do not underline; do not stack bold and italic.
- **Punctuation:** one space after a full stop; the serial comma is optional but
  must be consistent within a passage.

## 8. Worked examples — before / after

**Hero statement**

- *Before:* "If My people will pray… Executing the Mandate. Advancing the
  Kingdom. **It is NOW!**"
- *After:* "**"…then will I hear from heaven … and will heal their land."**
  — 2 Chronicles 7:14 (KJV). This house stands on that word. Executing the
  Mandate. Advancing the Kingdom. It is now."

**A claim about prayer**

- *Before:* "Prayer isn't a warm-up — it's amazing and it changes everything!!"
- *After:* "This house holds that prayer is its first work, not its last resort.
  Scripture affirms it: *'The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man
  availeth much'* — James 5:16 (KJV)."

**An invitation**

- *Before:* "Come check us out, it's gonna be awesome!"
- *After:* "The house convenes for Breakthrough Prayer Night each Friday at
  7:00 PM. You are welcome to stand with us."

## 9. The editor's checklist

Before copy is published, confirm each line:

1. Does every quotation carry a precise reference **and** a translation tag?
2. Is every quotation exact, with omissions honestly marked by an ellipsis?
3. Have exclamation marks and ALL-CAPS emphasis been removed (signature line
   excepted, §5)?
4. Does each paragraph read as a stated position rather than a plea?
5. Are all names, titles, dates, and the IPA registration reproduced exactly
   (§6)?
6. Is the spelling Commonwealth English throughout?
7. Could this passage stand in the public record of an institution without
   apology?
